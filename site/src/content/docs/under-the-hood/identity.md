---
title: How an idea keeps its identity
description: Stable IDs, typed events, and the rules for when an idea becomes a new one.
---

The hard question for an idea tracker is when something is still the same idea. Scintle answers it with one rule: **the same problem keeps the same ID.**

## The rules

- **Renamed** or **narrowed** (same problem, new name or smaller scope): same ID.
- **Pivoted** (the problem changed and the old idea is left behind): a new ID, linked from the old one. The old idea ends as *evolved*.
- **Spawned** (a new idea branched off while the first carries on): a new ID, linked; both stay alive.
- **Merged** (two ideas became one): a new ID with both as parents. Scintle never does this by itself; it [asks](/scintle/using/questions/).
- **Silence never means death.** An idea nobody mentions for months is still open until you, or a session, end it.

## The lifecycle

seed → exploring → planned → building → released, with **parked** possible at any stage, and **dropped** as the other way to end. A revived idea keeps its ID and picks up where it stopped.

## Events, not edits

An idea's title, problem and stage are never edited directly. They're worked out from its events: every rename, decision, plan and drop is an event with a time, a quote and a source. Corrections are events too, which is why every one can be undone, and why the board can show you what you knew at any past moment.
